# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person on the other side is trying to help, and read their message
that way before replying.

## What is expected

- **Judge the work, not the person.** "This misses the case where the preflight returns 4xx"
  is useful. "This is sloppy" is not.
- **Be patient with people who are still learning.** Everyone was new to HTTP semantics once,
  and a network tool attracts people learning the network.
- **Say why.** A change turned down with a reason teaches something; one turned down without
  a reason only stings.
- **English or French, whichever you are more comfortable with.** Both are read here. Do not
  let language be the reason you stay silent — a badly worded bug report is worth far more
  than a bug report never filed.

## What is not

Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments. Sexualised language or imagery. Publishing
someone's private information. Sustained disruption of discussions. Any of the above
directed at someone for who they are — their background, identity, appearance, beliefs,
experience level, or anything else that has nothing to do with the code.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space where someone is
representing this project.

## Reporting

Write to **erzneoz@gmail.com**. Reports are read by the maintainer and kept private.

Tell me what happened, where, and when. You do not need to have a solution in mind, and you
do not need to be the person it happened to.

What follows depends on what happened: a private word is often enough, and it can go as far
as a permanent ban. Whoever reports something in good faith will not be penalised for it,
whatever the outcome.
